# Product mission and acceptance criteria

**Current Antenna mission (7 November 2026, user scope correction): rescue-first resilient collective robotics with rice-field work as the measurable domain.** Local sensing, shared mapping, physically executed planting and safe navigation are useful existing tasks, but the core innovation is recognising robot failure, assessing helper risk/urgency and physically assisting or safely abstaining. The newly merged rigid-docking test is *scripted extraction mechanics*, not integrated autonomous recovery. **Antenna** is the canonical name (D-0008); **PaddyPatch** is its earlier name. D-0009 supersedes the previous rescue-optional priority.

## Real human problem and agronomic boundary

**Who benefits:** operators of direct-seeded rice fields with **patchy emergence or failed crop stands**, who need to decide *which missing areas to reseed*, where soil/water conditions and accessibility permit it, and how to cover those areas without damaging the healthy stand. This is **supplemental patch-level replanting** after identifying an uneven stand, **not a claim that a small robotic swarm outperforms existing mechanised broadcast seeding or transplanting of the whole field**. In a mostly uniform, well-established field, conventional treatment may be cheaper; the rational action might be *no reseeding*.

**Prior art and limitation:** [IRRI rice establishment guidance](https://www.knowledgebank.irri.org/images/docs/planting-establishment.pdf) explains planting/seeding depth, field conditions and crop establishment. The [IRRI Rice Production Manual](https://www.knowledgebank.irri.org/images/docs/planting-establishment.pdf) explains how seedbed, sowing depth, seed and site conditions affect establishment; its guidance is a reason to model **site-dependent inputs**, not a validated automatic best-planting-spot equation. [A separate precision rice seeding platform study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2025.1712934/full) reinforces that robotic precision planting itself is not novel. [Nguyen et al. (2025)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168169925010385) already fuse robot/RF/probe measurements into a high-resolution soil moisture map, and [multi-robot Kriging-variance route allocation (2025)](https://www.mdpi.com/2218-6581/14/6/77) already combines soil mapping with informative team routes. **Do not claim soil mapping, spatial interpolation or precision planting is unprecedented.** Test whether jointly choosing *where to sample, which gap to fill and how to route a real planter* adds benefit under limited seeds/time and changed conditions.

**Agronomic scope:** this is a **synthetic early-establishment gap-filling thought experiment**. Delayed reseeding may be inappropriate once the healthy stand is advanced; actual crop age, cultivar, irrigation, field preparation, costs and whether the right intervention is replanting rather than doing nothing would require agricultural validation. If conventional uniform/whole-field sowing wins on realistic total cost, we should **not** claim our robot is superior.

**Field choice:** a small **prepared, dry/direct-seeded or drained rice plot** with safe rover lanes, deliberately irregular established seedlings, missing patches and spatially changing moisture/compaction. The user has asked for plausible farming; **do not deploy a generic wheeled rover deep into a flooded paddy**, assert water can be read from image colour alone, or equate every damp/low patch with failure. Restoration timing and actual field suitability depend on variety, crop age, soil, water management and agronomic advice. The hackathon uses a deliberately simplified **synthetic establishment suitability/planting-benefit model**, not real crop-growth prediction.

## Rescue-first P0 acceptance — superseding the planting-only scope

**Existing useful mission:** a micro-field of 3–6 candidate rice sites, local location-gated synthetic soil/stand samples, public no-go areas, physical rovers and seed-proxy placement. Keep the field, planting physics and all source tests; measured rice yield, deep mud physics and real weather remain unvalidated.

**P0-R1 / perceive a real problem:** inject a **physical stall or mechanism failure** and identify it from commanded-vs-observed motion, sensor validity and time. A missing `front_range_m` or ordinary safe HOLD is not equivalent to immobilisation.

**P0-R2 / reason about intervention:** calculate capability, travel/traction risk, second-casualty probability, remaining fieldwork value, uncertain soil/weather context and qualitative operator urgency. Choose investigate, self-recover, dispatch help, defer, continue work or escalate. Compare to never rescue, always rescue and a competent calibrated deterministic risk optimiser; a cloud LLM is not required.

**P0-R3 / physically act:** the helper uses an **approved physically simulated docking/push/tow mechanism** to change the disabled rover's state, or safely refuses. PR #24 already provides a **scripted rigid docking, cam-latch, pitch-lift and tandem extraction MJCF test** demonstrating >0.08 m backward victim displacement; this must be connected to genuine incident perception, a bounded action/skill interface and an actual outcome/mission continuation. The controller is **not learned** unless genuine training and held-out evaluation are supplied.

**P0-R4 / independent judge proof:** physically meaningful fault → decision → bounded intervention or justified abstention → independent contact/displacement/helper-risk grade → resumed useful local mapping/planting where successful. Use matched incident seeds, a continuous ≤60-second demo and one-page PDF only for an actually executed integration. Do not equate a task reason string with rescue completion.

**Beyond P0:** independent partial per-robot maps, P2P radio gossip/consensus, learned recovery control, an offline small LLM and one larger LLM sharded across **verified** Jetson computers. None has been proven in this team and they are not prerequisites for safe motion.

**Historical P0:** planting-only milestones and the RiceWatch predecessor remain in prior D-0005–D-0007 decisions; they are preserved as actual development history, not deleted or misrepresented.

## Software seams, human decisions, and risk

**Proposed independent blocks:** risk-aware recovery supervisor and bounded physically verified rescue skill; physically meaningful simulator/sensor adapter; on-robot bounded controller; shared sample/map aggregator; mission-level target/route planner; planting actuator adapter; hidden-truth evaluator; read-only field/map UI. The UI can disappear without changing decisions; hidden cell quality never leaks to robots. The agent component chooses *what* to sample/plant; deterministic controllers decide *how* to move/release. An LLM is optional and must beat a capable interpolation + route/assignment optimiser on matched information and effort; don't sell a generic LLM as the novelty.

**Five human owners:** (1) integrator/schema/runner, (2) **human-led robot mechanics + physical planter actuator**, (3) field soil/sensor simulator and second scout, (4) shared map + site/route/task allocation, (5) independent evaluation, baselines and UI/video. Work in focused single-owner feature PRs **after one tested simulator contract**. [TEAM](TEAM.md) has exact assignments; [ARCHITECTURE](ARCHITECTURE.md) owns runtime; [CONTRACTS](CONTRACTS.md) owns the proposed interface; [RUN](RUN.md) owns runnable evidence.

**Critical feasibility risk:** placing even one physical seed proxy and making distinct soil observations from two moving robots may exceed the available hackathon time. First prioritise a minimal seed drop and one scout, **not** full seedling manipulation, soil continuum mechanics, watery rover dynamics, a large farm, multi-GPU deployment or RL training. If only one robot works, report a single-robot result rather than misrepresenting it as swarm coordination.

## Comparison and grading

**Baselines on identical worlds/seeds/seed budgets:** (A) conventional uniform/grid treatment on pre-defined gaps without targeted soil sampling; (B) competent single-rover local scoring and obstacle-aware route planner; (C) two-rover shared-map **deterministic** interpolation + assignment; (D) optional agentic active sample/replan policy. Compare the proposed method against C before attributing gains to higher-level intelligence. Also show when indiscriminate reseeding is better; don't engineer a toy baseline to guarantee our success.

**Metrics:** real planted seed proxies landing in valid missing-stand locations; synthetic marginal establishment benefit per seed and per metre; unnecessary/redundant probes; map error/uncertainty versus *hidden synthetic truth in independent evaluator*; completed good planting patches; covered gap fraction; collision/crop trampling/no-go violations; actor utilisation, route length, task delay, failed drops and mid-run adaptation latency. Use actual run traces, distinguish simulation proxies from field yield.

**Hackathon judges:** [EVENT](EVENT.md) confirms perception-driven action **25%**, adaptation **25%**, relevance/impact **20%**, operational autonomy **15%**, ambition/technical quality **15%**. Deliver a continuous ≤60s single-episode simulation recording with live sensor/map/action evidence, a surprise and the physical seed placement; one-page technical PDF; five-minute pitch if shortlisted. Submission remains a human-authorised action only.

## Embodied Robotics implementation gate — revealed 7 November 2026

**Challenge constraints are now known:** see [EVENT](EVENT.md) (organiser slides 35–38, full 100% rubric now confirmed from a clearer attendee photo), [independent evaluation](TESTING.md), and [fast/slow model / relevant prior-art research](RESEARCH.md#robotics-decision-model-research-fast-execution-slow-investigation-7-nov-2026). The rice-field mission above is the selected direction; the exact robot stack and testable scope must still be implemented, and it must be judged by the same objective as a competent non-LLM alternative.

| Gate | Team must be able to show | Do not accept as proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Perception-driven action** | An actual simulated robot consumes at least one simulated sensor and visibly selects an action based on the observation; new observations change later decisions | UI-only chat, privileged simulator coordinates disguised as sensor readings, pre-rendered animation |
| **Adaptation** | Within **one continuous <=60s episode**, an unexpected scene/sensor/goal change is detected and elicits bounded replanning, new measurement, or safe refusal; record the before/after trace | Operator switching prerecorded tracks or merely logging “obstacle detected” |
| **Operational autonomy** | Core step decisions and safety run with a local policy/controller; identify which reasoning/infrastructure can be remote and demonstrate bounded loss-of-network behavior | Remote hosted model on every servo tick, or asserting “offline” because the remote simulator streams video |
| **Relevance and impact — 20% of rubric** | A named human beneficiary with **specific supporting evidence** (firsthand need, actual operational failure or credible documented source), quantitative/observable benefit, and a **critical failure mode handled visibly in the demo** | Imaginary customer quotes, a generic “robots are useful” pitch, or just causing a failure without a safe response |
| **Ambition and technical quality — 15%** | Nontrivial technical barrier, original or meaningfully novel application, reliable engineering and measured trade-offs | Claiming a standard A*/RL/VLM/two-model loop is inherently innovative |
| **Submission proof** | One-page technical PDF, <=60s continuous simulation video with perception beside actions and a mid-run surprise; 5-minute pitch **if shortlisted** | Synthetic benchmark fixture, paper's reported score or a slide without actual robot action |

**Fast-versus-slow is not the idea:** A specialised/reactive controller, compact learned policy or bounded System One decision model can handle routine high-rate choices. A deliberative agent earns its role only when **active investigation, ambiguous diagnosis or mission-level adaptation** outperforms classical replanning, probabilistic inference or fixed experiment selection. [REA (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42053) already uses System 0/1/2 layers; [robojev bench](https://robo-jev.dev/) already compares typed decision models in robotic simulations. Do not sell either architecture as unprecedented.

**Minimum before committing to an implementation stack:** name one real person/organisation and mission with source-qualified evidence for why it matters, one feasible actuated robot scene + its real sensor channel, a mid-run disturbance not hardcoded into the controller, one strong classical baseline on the same seeds, independent non-LLM success/unsafe-action checks, run/recording feasibility on actual compute, and a known failure/abstention path. **The direction is selected; coordinate actual interfaces and team ownership before parallel implementation.** A remote Jev account, NVIDIA/Modal provision, correct physical sensor drivers or any model performance remain unverified.
