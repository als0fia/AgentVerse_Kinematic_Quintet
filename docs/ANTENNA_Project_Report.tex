% --- 1. ABSTRACT ---
\section{\textbf{Abstract: Overview \& Purpose}}
\textbf{Rice} feeds over half the world, yet planting in muddy fields still relies on an aging workforce—harming their health, threatening sustainability, and slowing harvests. \textbf{Antenna} is a team of small robots (\textbf{``Ennas''}) that tests the soil, plants missing rice to maximize yield, and smartly recovers stuck robot units to maximize farm output.

% --- 2. SOCIO-ECONOMIC FACTORS & RURAL CONTEXT ---
\section{\textbf{Why It Matters: Socio-Economic Need}}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Labor \& Mechanization Deficit:} Aging farmers and rising wages (\pounds 22--\pounds 40/day) threaten rushed planting windows ($<10\,\text{days}$). While national tillage exceeds 90\%, rice planting mechanization is only \textbf{67.6\%} (MARA 2024/2026; CSAE 2025), with severe shortages in Southern mountainous regions with small, fragmented fields ($<0.15\,\text{ha}$).
    \item \textbf{Why Current Machines Fail \& Why Antenna Wins:} Heavy transplanters (700--1,000\,kg) sink in 15--50\,cm mud pans, requiring 4--6 workers to pull out while crushing crops. Antenna provides affordable robotic farming services to China's $\approx$80,600 agricultural cooperatives at \textbf{\pounds 6.50--\pounds 10 per mu} (vs.\ \pounds 17--\pounds 25 for manual labor), supported by 30--35\% government hilly-machinery subsidies.
\end{itemize}

% --- 3. HARDWARE EMBODIMENT ---
\section{\textbf{The Robot Hardware: Lightweight ``Enna'' Design}}
Every swarm unit shares an identical \textbf{``Enna''} architecture: onboard edge computing (Jetson Orin Nano), soil-sensing and planting tools, and a rigid rescue hitch:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Compact Low-Pressure Hull:} A lightweight (\textbf{4.2\,kg}), waterproof body on gripped paddle wheels that floats over soft mud without sinking, built narrow enough (\textbf{276\,\text{mm}} wide) to drive cleanly inside standard \textbf{300\,\text{mm}} rice rows without crushing plants.
    \item \textbf{Onboard Tools:} Equipped with a plunging soil probe to test mud quality, a gravity-fed seed dropper, \textbf{3-beam} forward laser distance sensors, and a front lifting hitch that locks onto stuck teammates to pull them free.
\end{itemize}

% --- 4. SENSORS & ACTUATORS ---
\section{\textbf{The Hardware: Sensors \& Actuators}}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sensors (Perceive):} (1)~\textbf{3-Beam LiDAR} (front obstacles \& terrace walls); (2)~\textbf{Whiskers} (crop stem touch for row centering); (3)~\textbf{RTK + Odometry} (position \& wheel-slip stall detection); (4)~\textbf{Cone Penetrometer} (mud firmness, moisture, \& missing plant gaps); (5)~\textbf{P2P Radio} (local SOS \& map sharing within $2.15\,\text{m}$).
    \item \textbf{Actuators (Act):} (1)~\textbf{4-Wheel Drive} (skid-steer motion at $\pm 0.45\,\text{m/s}$); (2)~\textbf{Farming Tools} (seed-drop valve \& underbelly weeders); (3)~\textbf{Rescue Tools} (front lifting boom to break mud suction, rotary cam-latch lock, \& rear anchor spades).
\end{itemize}

% --- 5. PERCEPTION & STALL DETECTION ---
\section{\textbf{Perceive: Soil Checks \& Stall Detection}}
Ennas rely strictly on their own onboard sensors:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Checking Soil Before Planting:} Each robot probes soil moisture ($0.25\text{--}0.80$), firmness ($0.20\text{--}0.90$), and empty plant spacing ($\geq 0.60$). Validated planting spots are shared directly with nearby teammates over local radio.
    \item \textbf{Detecting When Stuck in Mud:} If a robot commands its wheels to drive ($\geq 0.04\,\text{m/s}$) with no obstacle ahead ($>0.32\,\text{m}$), yet barely moves forward ($<0.005\,\text{m}$) or turns ($<0.02\,\text{rad}$), it flags that it is bogged down in mud.
\end{enumerate}

% --- 6. AI AGENT ---
\section{\textbf{Decide: AI Agent}}
Our system pairs a Small Language Model with physics learning to recover trapped Ennas:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SLM Strategist + Residual Neural Learner:} An on-edge Small Language Model (\textbf{Qwen 2.5 0.5B}) acts as the high-level strategist, querying an online \textbf{Residual Neural Learner} to calculate real-world mud friction and wheel sinkage before deciding to self-push or call a teammate for a tow.
    \item \textbf{Modal GPU Training \& Edge Efficiency:} Fine-tuned on \textbf{Modal's cloud GPUs} in under five minutes, achieving \textbf{97.88\% accuracy} on unseen test scenarios within a \textbf{10-Watt edge power budget}.
    \item \textbf{Hardcoded Safety Gate \& 2\,ms Fallback:} Every AI decision passes through a \textbf{hardcoded safety gate} that blocks unsafe commands and protects battery limits, backed by an instant \textbf{2-millisecond automatic fallback}.
\end{itemize}

% --- 7. SMART SWARM RESCUE ---
\section{\textbf{Act: Smart Swarm Rescue}}
When a robot gets trapped in soft mud ($<30\,\text{kPa}$), the mud's suction makes it difficult to escape. Sending another robot without assessing the conditions could trap both, causing a \textbf{critical failure} known as cascade entrapment.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Detecting and Communicating the Problem:} The trapped robot detects its lack of movement, stops its wheels, estimates the mud resistance, and sends an SOS through local P2P radio to nearby teammates without needing internet.
    \item \textbf{Evaluating Rescue Risks:} The swarm calculates whether rescuing the robot is worth the risk, considering its remaining seeds, the time lost from planting, and the danger of another robot getting stuck:
    $$\text{Net} = \text{Rescue Benefit} - \text{Opportunity Cost} - \text{Hazard Risk}$$
    \item \textbf{Making a Safe Decision:} If $\text{Net} > 0$, the nearest available robot attempts a rescue while the others continue planting. If $\text{Net} \le 0$, the swarm refuses rescue to prevent further robots from getting trapped, prioritising the remaining mission.
\end{itemize}

% --- 8. PHYSICAL RESCUE ---
\section{\textbf{Physical Rescue: How One Robot Pulls Out Another}}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tandem Docking \& Suction Break:} The rescuer aligns into the stuck robot's rear V-funnel and snaps its rotary cam-latch shut to lock both robots into a rigid 8-wheel train. It then tilts its front boom upward ($+Z$) to pry the stuck robot up and break mud suction while dropping rear anchor spades before pulling backward.
    \item \textbf{MuJoCo Physics Validation:} Verified $>0.08\,\text{m}$ extraction pull within safe force limits ($<250\,\text{N}$, tilt $<0.12\,\text{rad}$), followed by immediate mission resumption.
\end{itemize}

% --- 10. OPERATING LIMITS ---
\section{\textbf{Operating Limits \& Real-World Scope}}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mud Depth Limit:} Effective in mud up to $\le 45\,\text{cm}$ deep. Deeper liquid slurry ($>60\,\text{cm}$) exceeds 8-wheel traction, triggering safe refusal for human pickup.
    \item \textbf{Simulation vs.\ Field Trials:} Multi-body docking and P2P radio are validated in 3D MuJoCo; wet clay slurry and field trials with co-ops are scheduled for Phase-2.
    \item \textbf{Target Farm Size:} Designed for gap-replanting in small hilly terraces ($<0.15\,\text{ha}$), complementing rather than replacing large tractors on flat plains.
\end{itemize}

% --- 11. SAFETY & OPERATIONAL RESILIENCE ---
\section{\textbf{Safety \& Operational Resilience}}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Operator UI Emergency Stop:} The mission dashboard features a continuous, real-time manual E-Stop that instantly overrides AI policies, cuts motor torque, and locks all chassis brakes in $<2\,\text{ms}$.
    \item \textbf{Environmental Ingress Protection (IP68):} Sealed waterproof hull with a positive-pressure air cavity protects internal electronics, LiDAR wiring, and batteries across saturated 15--50\,cm flooded paddy mud pans (\textit{Lannitian}), topped by an elevated splash-proof BeiDou RTK dome.
    \item \textbf{Zero Prompt Injection (Air-Gapped Edge AI):} All models (Qwen 2.5 0.5B and Residual Learner) execute fully on-device on the 10W Jetson chip with zero remote API dependencies or external text interfaces, completely eliminating cyber prompt-injection and cloud-hijacking attack surfaces.
    \item \textbf{Decentralized Mesh (No Single Point of Failure):} Fully peer-to-peer (P2P) mycelial gossip over $2.15\,\text{m}$ ad-hoc links eliminates central server bottlenecks. If an Enna gets stranded or loses power, surviving peers reallocate tasks autonomously without fleet-wide disruption.
\end{itemize}
